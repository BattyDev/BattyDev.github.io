Shadowbringers · the first Eden tier

Eden's Gate, normal, with seven other people

Four fights at level 80: Eden Prime, Voidwalker, Leviathan and Titan. This is the eight-player version — for running them alone at current level, read the unsynced guide instead.

The short version

Nothing in this tier is a damage check except one add phase in E1. Everything else is a positioning test, and almost every wipe comes from the same three things: standing in a puddle nobody soaked, being on the wrong half of a floor that just split, or getting knocked off an edge.

What you are walking into

E4 · SepultureTitanGeocrush and Fault Zone pushing you off the tiles

Getting in

  • Level 80, minimum item level 425, full light party: two tanks, two healers, four DPS.
  • Unlock the series after the Shadowbringers main scenario, by taking In the Middle of Nowhere from the Anxious Crystarium Guard in the Crystarium (X:9.8 Y:12.4). That leads to Ryne in the Empty, and Deploy the Core opens E1. Each later fight has its own quest in the same chain.
  • Entry is unlimited. Reward eligibility is once per fight per week and resets Tuesday. The fights drop tokens rather than gear — trade them to Yhal Yal in Eulmore (X:10.0 Y:11.5) or Ghul Gul in Amh Araeng (X:26.6 Y:16.4).

How to read the diagrams

Dot colours are consistent across every picture in both guides: main tank, off tank, healer, DPS. Red is ground that hurts, green is ground that does not, amber is the thing you are being asked to look at. The diagrams animate; if your system asks for reduced motion they hold still on the resolved position instead.

Cast timings quoted in this guide are pulled from the encounters' own timelines, so they are the game's numbers rather than a stopwatch. Treat them as ordering and spacing, not as a countdown to sync to.

Before the mechanics

What each role owns across the tier

Normal-mode raids flatten a lot of role identity — most mechanics ask everyone to stand somewhere. These are the parts that do not flatten.

Main tank Every fight

You are the fixed point. Keep the boss still and pointed away from the party, and eat the first half of every tankbuster.

Main tank
Three of the four bosses want to be parked and left alone — Eden Prime, Voidwalker and Leviathan barely need moving. Titan is the exception and he moves himself. Call your own swaps out loud; nobody else can see your debuff timer.
Off tank
Your job is mostly Provoke on cue and a second set of cooldowns. The two places you are load-bearing: the second Guardian in E1, and being the tank on the far shelf in E3.
DPS
Stay off the tanks' flanks in E1 — the Vice of Vanity lines fire down their tethers. Otherwise the tanks are your reference point for where "behind the boss" and "in the hitbox" are.
Healer
Both tanks take a hit in E2 at once. Everywhere else it is one at a time, and the vulnerability stack means the second hit lands on a fresh tank — so the shield goes on the incoming tank, not the one who just took it.

Named raidwides worth pre-mitigating Raidwide

The tier hits the whole party on a schedule, and the schedule is the same every pull. These are the ones where a cooldown planned in advance is worth more than a heal after.

DPS

Healer
Shield before, not heal after. The dangerous pattern in every one of these fights is a raidwide landing while half the party is still standing in something.

Who carries markers Every fight

Marked mechanics in this tier mostly fall on DPS and healers, and mostly ask for the same two shapes: get away from everyone, or get on top of everyone.

Main tank
You rarely carry one, which makes you the anchor. Stay where you are and let the spreads move around you — a boss that walks during a spread breaks four people's positions at once.
Off tank
When a stack marker goes out you are the easiest body to find. Be in it; a tank in the stack is free mitigation for it.
DPS
You carry most of them: Vice of Apathy in E1, Dark Fire III in E2, Drenching Pulse and Splashing Tsunami in E3. Fixed spread spots beat improvising every time.
Healer
You get your share, and yours are the ones that go wrong — a healer who runs out for a spread and keeps running is a healer out of range for the raidwide behind it.

Eden's Gate: Resurrection

E1 · Eden Prime

A square platform with a lethal edge, and a boss that stands in the middle of it doing one thing at a time. The opening two minutes teach you every mechanic the fight has.

The opening loop

  • 0:12Spear of Paradise → Heavensunder two-part tankbuster
  • 0:22Eden's Gravity raidwide, takes most of your health bar
  • 0:34Vice of Apathy → Eden's Flare puddles out, then everybody in
  • 0:56Vice of Vanity tank tethers
  • 1:07Spear of Paradise → Heavensunder
  • 1:26Pure Light get behind
  • 1:42Delta Attack three spells at once
  • 1:55Dimensional Shift raidwide, the floor changes

Spear of Paradise → Heavensunder Tankbuster

Two hits in sequence on the current target. The first applies a physical vulnerability, and Heavensunder lands a second and a half later — into that vulnerability if the same tank is still holding.

Main tank
Either take both and burn an invulnerability, or hand it over. The swap is cleaner: it costs nothing and it keeps your invuln for a mistake later.
Off tank
Provoke during the cast, not after the first hit — after is already too late for a clean swap. Have a mitigation up when you take it.
DPS
Free uptime. The boss does not move and nothing else is happening; this is where positionals get used.
Healer
Two separate tanks take one hit each if they swap. Top the outgoing tank on the swap — the vulnerability sticks to them, and the next thing out is a raidwide.

Vice of Apathy → Eden's Flare Spread, then stack

Four players get a puddle marker. The puddle stays where you drop it. Then Eden's Flare burns everything outside Eden Prime's hitbox — so the same four have to drop and come straight back in.

Main tank
Do not move. You are the hitbox everyone is running back to, and the hitbox is wherever the boss is standing.
Off tank
Stay in melee. If a DPS panics and drops a puddle short, being already inside makes it obvious to them where inside is.
DPS
Take a diagonal, not a cardinal — four puddles on the diagonals leave the cardinals clear for the run back. Melee can drop at max melee range, but agree on that first; a puddle at max melee is one someone else will clip.
Healer
You are not marked, so you do not move. Stay in the hitbox and watch for whoever drops theirs on top of another — that is the one you will be healing.

Vice of Vanity Tank lines

Eden Prime tethers both tanks and fires a line down each tether. Whatever is behind the tank is in the line.

Main tank
Take your tether to one edge and keep the boss where it is. Pick a side before the pull so you and the off tank are not both walking the same way.
Off tank
The opposite edge. You are tethered even though you are not tanking, so do not assume the marker is a mistake.
DPS
Get off the axis the tanks are on. Melee: step to the side of the boss the tanks are not using and keep hitting.
Healer
Two tanks take a real hit at the same time from opposite sides of the room. This is the moment in E1 where a single-target heal is not enough.

Pure Light Get behind

A ten-second cast that covers almost the entire platform in front of Eden Prime. The safe ground is a narrow wedge directly behind it.

Main tank
You decide where behind is. Point the boss at a wall you have agreed on and hold it there so seven people do not each pick a different rear.
Off tank
Go behind with everyone. The cast is long enough that you do not need to sprint — running past the edge is a worse outcome than arriving late.
DPS
Ranged should move in, not out. The wedge is narrow at the boss and wider at the edge, but the edge kills, so the safe band is thinner than it looks.
Healer
Move early and keep casting from behind. If someone is caught, they are probably dead rather than hurt — hold the resurrection until Delta Attack resolves.

Eden's Gate: Resurrection

E1 · Delta Attack, and the platform in space

The back half of E1 has the one mechanic that reliably wipes a first clear, and the one damage check in the tier.

Delta Attack Three at once

Three spells resolve together. Eden's Blizzard III is a circle centred on the boss. Eden's Thunder III is a cross through the middle row and column. Eden's Fire III is a spread marker on all eight players. The circle and the cross leave the four corner quadrants, and the spreads mean you cannot all pile into one.

The common wipe

Everyone dodges the cross correctly, three people end up in the same corner, and the Fire III circles overlap. Getting out of the cross is half the mechanic.

Main tank
Leave the boss in the middle and go to a corner yourself. Dragging it out of the centre moves the Blizzard circle onto the quadrant someone else is standing in.
Off tank
Take the corner diagonally opposite the main tank. Tanks in opposite corners is the easiest pattern for everyone else to fill in around.
DPS
One DPS to each corner, paired with a tank or healer, and stand at opposite ends of your quadrant. Your Fire III circle is bigger than the gap you think you have left.
Healer
Split up — both healers in the same corner is two spreads in one box. Heal on the move; the Fire III damage lands on everyone at once and Dimensional Shift is thirteen seconds behind it.

Dimensional Shift, Pure Beam, Paradise Lost Raidwide, then dodging

Dimensional Shift is a raidwide that changes the floor. Orbs then fire line attacks across the platform with Pure Beam, each aimed at whoever is nearest to it, while Paradise Lost drops circles under players three times over.

Main tank
Keep the boss central. The beams come from the outside, so a boss parked near an edge pins the melee against one.
Off tank
Nothing specific — dodge with the party. Save your party mitigation for Fragor Maximus, which is close behind.
DPS
Each orb picks the closest player, so spreading out evenly around the middle gives everyone a short line to sidestep rather than one person eating several.
Healer
Paradise Lost circles chase — keep moving and do not stop to hard-cast on top of one. The damage here is chip damage; the next cast is not.

Fragor Maximus → the Guardians Damage check

Fragor Maximus is a heavy raidwide, the boss goes untargetable, and the platform ends up in open space. Two Guardians of Paradise land and open with Paradisal Dive. They cleave with Mana Slice, hit the raid with Mana Burst, and about a minute after they appear they cast Unto Dust, which is the enrage. This is the only real damage requirement in the tier.

Main tank
Grab one immediately and turn it outward. Both adds land at once, so the pickup has to be instant — hold Provoke for whichever one strays.
Off tank
The other one, on the opposite side. This is the moment in E1 you exist for; a second Guardian loose in the party is a wipe on its own.
DPS
Split evenly or focus one then the other — either works, but decide. Burst goes here, not on the boss. Stay out of both cleave arcs while you do it.
Healer
Mana Burst hits the raid repeatedly on top of two tanks taking cleaves. Get everyone to full before the second Guardian dies, because Eternal Breath follows and it is the biggest hit in the tier.

Eternal Breath The big one

After the adds, a long scripted wind-up — Primeval Stasis — leads into Eden Prime's limit break. It is unavoidable and very large. Then the boss becomes targetable again and the whole fight from Vice of Vanity onward repeats until it dies.

Main tank
Party mitigation here, without question. Re-establish your grip the moment the boss is targetable — it re-opens with Vice of Vanity, then a tankbuster.
Off tank
Your mitigation too. Neither of you needs it for the adds, and both of you have it available.
DPS
Nothing to dodge. Use the downtime to get your cooldowns lined up for the next burst window rather than holding them through it.
Healer
Everything you have. Full party, shields up, and a heal queued for immediately after. Anyone below full when it lands will not be alive after it.

Eden's Gate: Descent

E2 · Voidwalker

A round arena over nothing, a boss with four or five simple attacks, and one mechanic that takes those simple attacks and makes you solve them out of order.

The opening loop

  • 0:11Doomvoid Guillotine band across the room — go to the sides
  • 0:28Doomvoid Slicer everything but the boss — get under
  • 0:47Shadowflame hits both tanks at once
  • 0:59Dark Fire III spread
  • 1:07Unholy Darkness stack
  • 1:13Spell-In-Waiting and from here nothing lands when it is cast
  • 2:37Entropy raidwide — marks the top of each block from now on

Doomvoid Guillotine and Doomvoid Slicer Opposite answers

Two casts with almost the same name and exactly opposite safe spots. Guillotine is a band across the whole arena, so you go to the sides. Slicer covers the whole room except a circle under Voidwalker, so you get underneath it. Each also leaves untargetable adds walking across the floor — Guillotine's march outward from the band, Slicer's march in from the edge.

Main tank
On Slicer you are already standing in the safe spot, so keep the boss still and let people come to you. On Guillotine, walking the boss is what pushes the safe sides out of shape — stay put.
Off tank
Follow the main tank's side on Guillotine so the party has one obvious half to fill rather than two.
DPS
Melee barely move for Slicer, which makes it free uptime. Ranged: Guillotine is the one that costs you, so pick your side early and finish the cast there.
Healer
The adds do not need to be dodged carefully, but they do chip. Watch for the player who reads "Doomvoid" and guesses — that is who takes the whole band.

Shadowflame Both tanks

Not a single-target buster. Shadowflame hits the top two on the enmity list at the same time, which in practice means both tanks.

Main tank
Personal mitigation every time. There is no swap that helps here — the off tank is being hit as well.
Off tank
Mitigate too, and stay on the enmity list. Dropping to third means a DPS takes a tankbuster instead of you.
DPS
Nothing to do, but do not open on the boss so hard at the pull that you climb above the off tank.
Healer
Two tanks, one cast. Plan a party shield or a pair of single-target mitigations rather than reacting to two health bars dropping together.

Dark Fire III, Unholy Darkness, Shadoweye The three markers

The whole fight is built out of these three, cast plainly at first and delayed later. Dark Fire III is a spread. Unholy Darkness is a stack on one player. Shadoweye is a gaze — everyone facing the marked player is petrified.

Main tank
On Shadoweye, turn your camera rather than your character if you can — the boss still needs facing away from the party.
Off tank
Be in the Unholy Darkness stack. A tank in a stack is the cheapest mitigation the party has for it.
DPS
Spread spots should be fixed, not chosen. Dark Fire III lands on several people at once and two spreads touching costs both of them.
Healer
Petrification from a missed gaze is not something you can heal through, and the person petrified will eat whatever lands next. Call the eye target out loud.

Empty Hate into Punishing Ray Knockback, then soak

The Hand of Erebos pushes everyone straight away from it with Empty Hate, and the arena edge is a fall. Punishing Ray puts five puddles on the floor that each need a body in them; any left empty deal damage and stack a Diabolic Curse damage-taken debuff on the party. The two come together, and the order is fixed: survive the push first, then soak.

Main tank
You choose where the party stands by where you put the boss. Keep it on the Hand's side of the arena so the push has the whole floor to travel across.
Off tank
Take a puddle. There are five and eight of you — the three spare bodies should be the ones with the least to lose from standing in a bleed.
DPS
Take the puddles the knockback drops you nearest to. Do not fight the push to reach a specific one; the puddles are interchangeable.
Healer
A soaked puddle applies a small bleed, which is fine. An unsoaked one applies Diabolic Curse to everyone, and the next raidwide is what kills them. Count the puddles, not the health bars.

Eden's Gate: Descent

E2 · Spell-In-Waiting

The mechanic the fight is actually about, and the only genuinely unusual thing in the tier.

The whole idea

After Spell-In-Waiting, Voidwalker's spells stop resolving when they are cast. A clock appears over the marked player instead, and the spell lands up to thirty seconds later — on top of whatever the boss is doing by then. Read the clock, not the cast bar.

How it actually plays

  1. A spell goes on the clock You get a countdown icon instead of the usual marker. Nothing happens yet.
  2. The fight carries on Doomvoid casts, Entropy, Empty Hate and Punishing Ray all keep coming, live.
  3. The clock runs out The delayed spell resolves now — spread, stack or gaze — usually while you are already solving something else.
  4. Two land together Later blocks put a delayed stack and a delayed spread on the clock at the same time. The stack and the spread have to happen in the same second, on different people.

Solving a delayed spell on top of a live one The whole fight

Every wipe in E2 is some version of this: somebody solved the cast bar and forgot the clock, or solved the clock and stood in the cast bar.

Main tank
Keep the boss parked in the middle. When a delayed stack lands during a Doomvoid cast, the party needs the boss where it has always been to know where "under" is.
Off tank
Be the stack point when a delayed Unholy Darkness is due. A predictable body to run to is worth more than anything else you could be doing in that second.
DPS
If you are holding a delayed spread, pre-position: drift to your spread spot early and keep attacking from there rather than sprinting when the clock hits zero.
Healer
Call the delayed gaze. Everyone is watching their own icon and nobody is watching whose eye is about to go off — that call is the single most useful thing said in this fight.
One awkward overlap

There is a point where a delayed stack resolves at the same moment as a spread. The spread markers must not be in the stack — if you are holding fire, get out even though everyone else is running in.

Eden's Gate: Inundation

E3 · Leviathan

A floating platform with no walls, a boss that spends the fight trying to push you off it, and one cast that takes the platform apart underneath you.

Assign this before you pull

Undersea Quake can cut the arena in half and leave the two halves unreachable from each other. Decide now: one tank and one healer on each side, and two DPS with each of them. Sorting it out after the floor is gone is not possible.

The opening loop

  • 0:13Tidal Roar raidwide, and it recurs constantly
  • 0:24Rip Current tankbuster, on a head marker
  • 0:38Temporary Current huge line across the platform from a corner
  • 0:47Drenching Pulse → Monster Wave → Freak Wave spread
  • 1:39Tidal Wave knockback from one edge
  • 1:46Undersea Quake the outer ring falls away
  • 1:55Crashing Pulse → Monster Wave → Killer Wave stack, on the smaller floor

Undersea Quake Two casts, opposite answers

The same name, twice, meaning opposite things. One version destroys the outer ring and leaves the middle — get in. The other destroys the middle and leaves the east and west shelves — go out, and pick a side, because you cannot cross afterwards.

Main tank
On the split version you take one shelf with the boss if it is on your side, and the party follows the boss. Say which side you are taking.
Off tank
The other shelf, every time. Your shelf has no boss and no tankbuster — it has two DPS and a healer who need someone calling the dodges.
DPS
Two per shelf, decided before the pull. Ranged can keep hitting across the gap; melee on the far shelf simply lose uptime, and that is fine.
Healer
One of you per shelf, no exceptions. Tidal Roar keeps landing while the floor is split and four people with no healer will not survive two of them.

Tidal Wave Knockback off the edge

Water rises on one edge of the platform and, a few seconds later, shoves everyone directly away from it. There is no wall to stop you.

Main tank
Arm's Length covers you and lets you keep the boss exactly where it was, which is worth more than the button.
Off tank
Same. Between the two of you there is no reason for either tank to ever be moved by this.
DPS
Arm's Length or Surecast if your job has it, otherwise walk towards the water during the cast. Do not stand in the middle and hope — the push clears the whole platform.
Healer
Surecast, and watch where the party ends up rather than where it started. Anyone who goes off the edge can be raised, but not quickly.

Temporary Current, Rip Current, and the Pulses The regular beats

Temporary Current is a very wide line fired across the platform from one of the front corners — find the corner it is not covering. Rip Current is the tankbuster, on a head marker. Drenching Pulse drops a circle under every player and then sends a travelling circle after each of them; Crashing Pulse does the same and follows with a stack marker.

Main tank
Rip Current is yours. It is a plain single-target hit — mitigate and hold, there is no vulnerability forcing a swap here.
Off tank
Free. Be in the Crashing Pulse stack and keep an eye on which corner Temporary Current leaves open, because the party will look at you for it.
DPS
On Drenching Pulse, bunch up during the cast so the first set of circles lands in one clump, then spread wide for the travelling ones. Spreading first leaves circles everywhere you need to run.
Healer
These land on an already-shrunken floor later in the fight. Keep the party's positions tight enough that a stack is reachable and loose enough that spreads do not overlap — that balance is mostly you calling it.

Eden's Gate: Inundation

E3 · The Tsunami suite and Maelstrom

Four markers in a row, then the only part of the fight where Leviathan leaves the platform and comes back through it.

The four Tsunami markers Back to back

Leviathan casts Tsunami and then throws four different markers out in quick succession. They are all simple; the difficulty is that they arrive faster than you can read them, so learn the shapes rather than the names.

Main tank
Hold the boss still through all four. Everyone is reading markers off each other's positions and a boss that walks moves the whole reference frame.
Off tank
Be findable. You are the default body for Smothering, and if you are carrying Surging, take the push towards the middle of the floor.
DPS
If you get Swirling, stay still and let people come to you — running with a donut on your head drags the safe spot away from everyone trying to reach it.
Healer
Call the donut. It is the only one of the four where the correct answer is counter-intuitive, and it is the only one that reliably kills someone.

Maelstrom and Spinning Dive Boss off the field

Leviathan goes untargetable, the floor fills with circles, and it dives back across the platform twice. Avoid the puddles and avoid the dive line — the two are laid out so that the obvious dodge for one puts you in the other.

Main tank
Nothing to hold. Pick up the boss the instant it is targetable again — Tidal Roar and Rip Current come straight after.
Off tank
Stay on the enmity list so the boss does not come back facing the party.
DPS
Real downtime. Do not waste a burst window on nothing — let cooldowns come back for when the boss returns.
Healer
Top everyone during the dives. The fight restarts its whole opening sequence the moment Leviathan lands.

Eden's Gate: Sepulture

E4 · Titan

A four-by-four grid of tiles and a boss who spends the fight deleting them or throwing you past them. Almost nothing here is damage — it is all footwork.

The one habit that clears this fight

Always know which tile you would step to next, and prefer the tile that just exploded. Ground that has already gone off is the safest ground on the floor.

The opening loop

  • 0:13Voice of the Land raidwide
  • 0:21Evil Earth chain of tiles
  • 0:31Weight of the Land circles under players
  • 0:42Stonecrusher tankbuster
  • 0:54Crumbling Down → Seismic Wave hide behind the boulder
  • 1:19Geocrush he lands, everyone flies
  • 1:23Earthen Gauntlets form change — see the next section

Evil Earth Chain reaction

One tile is marked and explodes. Then every tile touching it explodes. Then the ring beyond that. The wave moves outward, and a tile that has already gone off does not go off again.

Main tank
You are choosing where the melee stand, so keep the boss near the middle where there are tiles in every direction. A boss in a corner gives melee one escape route.
Off tank
Nothing specific — move with the melee and be visible. On the later loops this overlaps with the boulders and the party follows whoever moves first.
DPS
One step, not three. Melee lose almost no uptime here if they move onto the detonated tile instead of running to the edge.
Healer
Later in the fight Evil Earth arrives layered with Bomb Boulders and Weight of the Land. Do not try to heal through it — nobody should be taking it at all, and the ones who do are usually stuck in a corner.

Geocrush Knockback off the tiles

Arrows appear on one tile pointing outward, Titan jumps onto it, and everyone is pushed directly away. The distance is fixed, so the only variable is how much floor you have behind you.

Main tank
You are being pushed too, and you lose the boss position when it lands anyway — Titan moves himself. Take it from the inner side and re-establish after.
Off tank
Arm's Length works here. A tank who does not move is a landmark for everyone who did.
DPS
Get next to the marked tile on the side facing the rest of the arena. Ranged are the ones who die to this, because they start furthest out.
Healer
Geocrush is immediately followed by a form change, so be in position for that before you start healing the push damage.

Boulders, bombs and Seismic Wave Cover and order

Crumbling Down drops a Massive Boulder on the floor and Seismic Wave follows — the boulder is cover, so put it between you and Titan. Bomb Boulders scatter smaller rocks that explode in the order they landed, and Cobalt Bomb enlarges the one Titan is tethered to. Stand at the edge between two bombs, and once one has gone off, move onto the ground it left.

Main tank
Park Titan so there is boulder-shaped cover between him and the party. This is the one mechanic where where you put the boss decides whether the mechanic is easy.
Off tank
Stonecrusher opens this block. Take the swap if your group swaps, then get behind the same boulder as everyone else.
DPS
Melee: uptime is not worth it here — Seismic Wave is a wipe-shaped mistake and the boulder is not next to the boss. Take the loss.
Healer
Stay behind cover with the party. Being the one person still in the open because you were topping someone off is how this goes wrong.

Eden's Gate: Sepulture

E4 · Gauntlets and wheels

After each Geocrush, Titan puts something on. Which thing decides the next forty seconds, and the fight alternates between the two for the rest of its length.

Read the form, not the timer

Earthen Gauntlets means Landslides are coming. Earthen Wheels means Fault Zone is coming. The fight branches after the first pair and the order stops being predictable, so the form change is your only reliable cue. Earthen Armor at the end of each block is him taking it off again.

Earthen Gauntlets → Massive Landslide → Left / Right Landslide Front, then across

Massive Landslide has no telegraph — Titan swings behind and to both sides, so the safe ground is the strip in front of him. He follows with Left or Right Landslide, which erupts one half of the arena. The cast name is written from Titan's point of view, so watch the arm he winds up rather than the word.

Main tank
Your facing is the safe spot for Massive Landslide. Keep him pointed at a half of the arena with room in it and do not turn him mid-cast.
Off tank
Stand in front with the party. If you are swapping around Stonecrusher, do it before the gauntlets go on, not during.
DPS
In front of a boss is an odd place to be and it feels wrong — do it anyway. Melee keep full uptime through Massive Landslide.
Healer
Ranged habits kill you here: standing behind Titan is exactly the wrong side. Move up with the melee for this one.

Earthen Wheels → Fault Zone ×3 → Magnitude 5.0 Charges, then a donut

Titan lines up on a row and charges down it, three times, taking a new row each pass — and the charge knocks back as well as hitting. Stand on his flank, on the side with more tiles left. The block finishes with Magnitude 5.0, a donut whose only safe ground is against his hitbox.

Main tank
Titan drives himself, so you are not steering. Your job is to be on the correct flank first — people will follow you into the charge lane if you are wrong.
Off tank
Same flank, and do not be the one caught over open air when the knockback lands. The tiles are already thinner by this point in the fight.
DPS
Three passes means three repositions. Ranged: do not drift outward between them — Magnitude 5.0 is coming and you want to already be close.
Healer
Move in for Magnitude 5.0 early. It follows the third charge closely and a healer caught in the donut takes the party with them.

Earthen Fury Block break

A large raidwide with a damage-over-time attached, cast at the end of a full rotation of forms. It is the marker that the fight is looping rather than escalating.

Main tank
Party mitigation. Stonecrusher follows a few seconds later, so have a personal ready as well.
Off tank
Take the Stonecrusher swap after it if your group swaps — it is a clean, quiet window.
DPS
Feint or Addle it. Nothing needs dodging during the cast, which makes it the cheapest mitigation you will spend all fight.
Healer
The tail damage is what catches people — heal through the dot, not just the hit, because Bomb Boulders and Evil Earth start again on top of it.
