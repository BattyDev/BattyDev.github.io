Eden's Gate · alone, at current level

What unsynced actually changes

Every number in the tier stops mattering. Almost nothing else does. This guide is about the handful of mechanics that do not care how strong you are — for the eight-player version, read the full party guide.

The whole thing in one line

Unsynced deletes the damage, the healing and the damage check. It does not delete gravity. Every fight you can lose solo, you lose by being pushed off an edge or standing on a tile that stopped existing.

Getting in

  1. Have the duty unlocked Unsynced does not skip the unlock. Each of the four fights has its own quest in the Eden chain, starting from In the Middle of Nowhere in the Crystarium and Deploy the Core from Ryne in the Empty.
  2. Be in a party of one Duty Finder, on your own or as the leader of a premade. You are not matchmaking — this registers as a private party and will not fill.
  3. Tick Unrestricted Party It is in the duty's settings alongside the level sync and item level options. Leave the rest of the boxes as they are.
  4. Join Level sync and item level sync are lifted, and the party size requirement with them.

What is still true

  • Reward eligibility is unchanged. One token per fight per week, reset Tuesday, traded at Yhal Yal in Eulmore (X:10.0 Y:11.5) or Ghul Gul in Amh Araeng (X:26.6 Y:16.4). Entering as many times as you like has never been the restriction.
  • Nobody is going to raise you. A death is a run. Falling off in E3 or E4 is a death, not a knock and a teleport.
  • The bosses still have raid health bars. Expect several minutes each. All four fights loop their mechanics, so you will see the full rotation more than once — the later loops are the same mechanics arriving closer together, not new ones.

Before you queue

What to bring, and what it buys you

There is no wrong job for this. There are jobs that make the four dangerous mechanics into non-events.

One button matters more than your job

Arm's Length (tanks and melee) or Surecast (casters) makes you immune to every knockback in the tier. That is Empty Hate in E2, Tidal Wave in E3, and Geocrush and Fault Zone in E4 — four of the five ways to die solo, on one off-global-cooldown button. Healers have Surecast; physical ranged have Arm's Length.

Tank

The comfortable choice. The health pool absorbs every mistake, self-healing covers the chip damage, and Arm's Length is already on your bar. Slowest kill times of the three, which in a fight with no enrage is not a cost.

Healer

The safe choice. Nothing out-damages your own healing at this level gap, and Surecast covers the knockbacks. Kill times are long — expect the mechanic loops to come around several times per fight.

DPS

The fast choice. Fewest loops, least margin. Melee and physical ranged have Arm's Length; casters have Surecast. You still have more health than anything in this tier can spend, but you are the one who dies to two mistakes in a row.

What to actually put on the bar All four fights

Nothing exotic. The point is that the buttons that matter are the utility ones, not the rotation.

Tank
Arm's Length for the knockbacks, Sprint for Undersea Quake, and an invulnerability you will almost certainly not need. Skip the tank stance question entirely — nothing else is generating enmity.
Healer
Surecast and Sprint. Bring your damage-over-time and your filler; you will be pressing them for a long time and healing yourself roughly never.
DPS
Arm's Length or Surecast depending on your job, plus Sprint. Second Wind or its equivalent covers everything the tier will do to you in one fight.
Worth knowing

There is no Duty Support or Trust for raids — Unrestricted Party is the only way in alone, and it means genuinely alone. No NPC is going to soak a puddle for you.

Eden's Gate: Resurrection

E1 · Eden Prime

The easiest of the four alone. Its one damage check evaporates, and everything else is a boss standing still on a square platform.

What changes

Delta Attack stops being a mechanic. Eight people have to share four corners; you have one Fire III circle and four empty corners to put it in. The add phase's Unto Dust enrage is a minute-long window to kill two adds that will take you seconds.

Delta Attack Trivial alone

Blizzard III is a circle on the boss, Thunder III is a cross through the middle, Fire III is a spread on you. Walk to a corner. That is the entire mechanic when there is nobody to overlap with.

Tank
Leave the boss where it is and walk out. Dragging it moves the Blizzard circle with it, which is the only way to make this hard.
Healer
Free casting time in the corner. Nothing here threatens you.
DPS
Melee lose a few seconds of uptime. Ranged lose none — the corners are in range of a boss parked in the middle.

Pure Light Still means it

The one cast in E1 with an answer you cannot brute-force: it covers the platform except a wedge behind the boss, and running away from it means running off the edge. The damage will not kill you at level; the edge will.

Tank
You control the facing, so you already know where behind is. Turn the boss towards the middle of the platform before the cast and step round.
Healer
Move first, cast second. It is a long cast and you will not miss much.
DPS
Ranged: resist the instinct to back up. Distance is not the answer to this one and the platform is smaller than it looks.

Vice of Apathy, Eden's Flare, the Guardians Everything else

Drop your puddle somewhere you are not going to stand and get back inside the boss's hitbox for Eden's Flare — the shape still matters, the damage does not. The Guardians land, cleave at you, and die. Eternal Breath is a very large raidwide at level 80 and an inconvenience at yours.

Tank
Grab both Guardians at once; there is nobody else for them to walk to. They will be dead before Mana Burst matters.
Healer
The one place to keep an eye on your own bar is the Guardians' Mana Burst arriving on top of Eden's Gravity. "Keep an eye on" rather than "plan for".
DPS
Burst the adds so the phase ends early — it is the only part of the fight that costs you real time rather than boss health.

Eden's Gate: Descent

E2 · Voidwalker

Spell-In-Waiting is much easier alone — every delayed marker is yours, so there is never a question of whose. Two things still want attention.

Punishing Ray The one you cannot solve

Five puddles appear and each wants a body in it. Any left empty deal damage and apply Diabolic Curse, a damage-taken increase. Alone you can stand in exactly one of them, so plan to eat this — it is the only mechanic in the tier that a single player is structurally unable to complete.

Tank
Barely register it. Stand in the nearest puddle, take the other four, carry on.
Healer
This is your cue to heal yourself — the only one in the fight. The Curse stacks make the next Entropy noticeably larger.
DPS
Soak the closest one anyway; one fewer unsoaked puddle is one fewer stack. Use a personal mitigation if the timing lines up with Entropy.

Empty Hate The void is still the void

The Hand of Erebos pushes you directly away from it and the arena has no walls. This is the one thing in E2 that ends a run.

Tank
Arm's Length and forget it. Otherwise fight the boss from the Hand's side of the arena so the push has floor to cross.
Healer
Surecast. It is off the global cooldown and it turns the mechanic off entirely.
DPS
Ranged are the ones who die here, because you naturally stand at the far edge. Move in during the cast even if you are using the immunity.

Spell-In-Waiting, Doomvoid, Shadoweye Simpler alone

The clocks still run, but every clock is yours, so there is no coordination left in the mechanic — read your own icon and solve it. Doomvoid Guillotine means the sides; Doomvoid Slicer means under the boss. Shadoweye marks a player and petrifies anyone facing them, and alone the only player it can mark is you — turn the camera away out of habit, it costs nothing.

Tank
You are standing under the boss already, so Slicer is free. Guillotine is the one that moves you.
Healer
Nothing to manage. Watch your own clock icons and keep casting.
DPS
Melee get almost full uptime in this fight. Ranged should stay mid-range rather than max — every knockback in here is measured from the middle.

Eden's Gate: Inundation

E3 · Leviathan

The one to be careful in. Nothing here can out-damage you, and two things here can end the run in a second and a half.

The hardest fight in the tier to solo

Not because of anything Leviathan does to your health bar. Because it is the only fight that combines a platform with no walls, a knockback that clears the whole platform, and a cast that deletes the floor you are standing on.

Tidal Wave Number one cause of death

Water rises on one edge and pushes you directly away from it, far enough to clear the entire platform. Solo there is nobody to raise you.

Tank
Arm's Length. Its cooldown is comfortably shorter than the gap between Tidal Waves, so there is no reason to ever take one.
Healer
Surecast, every time. Treat it as part of the cast bar rather than a reaction.
DPS
Same button, whichever you have. If it is on cooldown, stop attacking and walk into the wave — the uptime is not worth the run.

Undersea Quake Two casts, opposite answers

One version destroys the outer ring and leaves the middle. The other destroys the middle and leaves the east and west shelves. Alone, the party-splitting problem disappears — but the floor disappearing does not, and guessing wrong is fatal rather than expensive.

Tank
If the boss ends up on the far shelf, you simply lose it for a while. That is fine — nothing is going to wander off and hit anyone.
Healer
Take the shelf the boss is on if you can, so your damage keeps landing. Being stranded is a time cost and nothing worse.
DPS
Melee want the boss's shelf. Ranged do not care — you can keep hitting across the gap.

Temporary Current, the Tsunami markers, Maelstrom Dodge and carry on

Temporary Current is a wide line from a corner — take a different corner. The Tsunami markers all land on you alone: the stack becomes a solo hit, the spread becomes a non-event, and only Swirling Tsunami, the donut, has an answer worth knowing, because safe is the ring's inside rather than away from it. Maelstrom is puddles and two dives, both of which are simply dodged.

Tank
Keep the boss near the middle of the floor for your own sake — every knockback in this fight is measured from where you are standing.
Healer
Smothering alone hits harder than it does in a party, since nothing is splitting it. It is still not enough to matter.
DPS
Maelstrom is real downtime. Let cooldowns come back rather than dumping them into an untargetable boss.

Eden's Gate: Sepulture

E4 · Titan

A shrinking floor and two different ways of being thrown off it. Bring the knockback immunity and this becomes a long, safe fight.

Geocrush Hug the landing

Arrows mark a tile, Titan lands on it, everything is pushed away. The distance is fixed, so the only thing you control is how much floor is behind you.

Tank
Arm's Length covers this and Fault Zone both. Between the two mechanics it will roughly line up with its own cooldown.
Healer
Surecast, or walk in. Do not try to out-heal being launched into open sky; it does not work that way.
DPS
Ranged: this is the one that catches you, every time, because you are always the furthest out. Close the distance during the telegraph.

Fault Zone → Magnitude 5.0 Flank, then get in

In his wheels form Titan charges down a row three times, taking a new row each pass, and the charge knocks back as well as hitting. Stand on his flank, on the side with more tiles left. Then Magnitude 5.0 is a donut and the only safe ground is against his hitbox — the answer flips from "get away" to "get on him" with no gap.

Tank
You are not steering him, so treat this as a dodge like anyone else. Being in melee already puts you in the right place for the donut.
Healer
Move in on the third charge rather than after it. The donut arrives faster than it reads.
DPS
Melee are already inside for Magnitude 5.0. Ranged need to make the trip, and the charges are what tell you it is coming.

Evil Earth, boulders and Landslides Footwork only

Evil Earth chains outward from one tile, so step back onto the tile that already exploded. Bomb Boulders detonate in the order they landed — follow the gaps. Massive Landslide has no telegraph and is safe only in front of Titan; Left or Right Landslide erupts one half, and the name is written from his point of view, so read the arm.

Tank
You still control his facing between form changes, which means you still control where "in front" is for Massive Landslide.
Healer
Ranged habits are the danger — behind Titan is the wrong side of Massive Landslide. Stand up front for the gauntlets blocks.
DPS
Melee barely move for any of this. If you are ranged, the whole gauntlets sequence wants you closer than feels natural.

Four fights, five real mechanics

Everything else in Eden's Gate is damage, and damage is not the problem any more.

Everything that stopped mattering

  • Delta Attack (E1) — one spread marker, four empty corners.
  • The Guardians and Unto Dust (E1) — a minute-long enrage window for two adds that die in seconds.
  • Eternal Breath (E1) — the biggest hit in the tier, and no longer close.
  • Shadowflame (E2) — a tankbuster with only one tank in the room.
  • Spell-In-Waiting (E2) — every clock is yours, so there is nothing to coordinate.
  • Splitting for Undersea Quake (E3) — no party to split.
  • Every stack marker — nothing to share it with, and nothing that needs sharing.

Cast names and ordering are taken from the encounters' own fight timelines as published in cactbot's raidboss data. The solo notes are reasoning about those mechanics at a large level gap rather than a claim about a specific job's numbers — if something here reads as more dangerous than you find it, trust your run.
